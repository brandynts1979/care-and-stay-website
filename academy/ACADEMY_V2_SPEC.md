# Academy 2.0 — approved direction and implementation specification

Status: development design, not published, not a billing or production change.
Date: 2026-11-24.

## Product purpose
Flagship organizational entry product: Attract. Thrive. Stay. The existing Seven Pillars are the organizational framework; the eight competencies are observable leadership behaviors. Retrieve the canonical pillar labels and Individual 360 dimension IDs before implementing mappings; do not invent or rename them.

## Core curriculum
Each lesson below has an objective, instructional principle, reflection and field assignment. Expand each into full instructional reading, fictional contextual scenario, practice simulation, knowledge check with feedback and accessibility-reviewed learning content before publishing.

| ID | Lesson | Instructional principle and observable objective | Reflection | Workplace application |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V2-01-01 | Leadership identity and purpose | Leadership is influence directed toward shared flourishing; distinguish authority from service and name a purpose beyond personal status. | When did my leadership help someone thrive? | Interview a colleague about a peak leadership experience. |
| V2-01-02 | Values and authenticity | Authenticity is congruence between values, words and behavior, not unfiltered expression; identify and repair a values-behavior gap. | What value is hardest to embody under pressure? | Choose one value and demonstrate it in three observable actions. |
| V2-01-03 | Servant leadership | Serve growth and autonomy while maintaining boundaries and standards; distinguish support from rescuing. | When have I helped someone become less dependent on me? | Ask a team member what support would enable greater ownership. |
| V2-01-04 | Personal leadership philosophy | Use discovery stories to design a concise leadership commitment and test it against real behavior. | What would colleagues say my philosophy actually is? | Draft and share a leadership commitment; invite feedback. |
| V2-02-01 | Emotional self-awareness | Separate emotion, interpretation, and observable fact; name triggers and body cues without judging them. | When have my emotions provided useful information? | Keep a trigger-response journal for five working days. |
| V2-02-02 | Self-regulation | Create space between stimulus and response; reappraise, pause, and choose an action aligned with values. | What helps me respond well during pressure? | Rehearse a pause-and-clarify routine before a difficult meeting. |
| V2-02-03 | Empathy and social awareness | Distinguish empathy from agreement; check interpretations through respectful questions. | When did someone understand me without agreeing? | Ask permission to explore a colleague's perspective and summarize it. |
| V2-02-04 | Intentional emotional leadership | Emotional tone influences team interactions; model calm, honesty and repair rather than demanding positivity. | What emotional climate do I create? | Conduct a check-in and invite feedback about team climate. |
| V2-03-01 | Listening to understand | Attend, reflect content and emotion, verify accuracy, and resist premature advice. | Who listens to me particularly well? | Practice five uninterrupted minutes of listening. |
| V2-03-02 | Powerful questions | Open questions expand possibilities; avoid disguised advice and leading questions. | What question changed my thinking? | Replace three directive statements with curious questions. |
| V2-03-03 | Appreciative conversations | Define a shared affirmative topic; discover exceptions and strengths; dream, design and sustain. | When have we already experienced the desired outcome? | Conduct an appreciative interview and co-create an experiment. |
| V2-03-04 | Expectations and purpose | Connect a clear observable standard to shared purpose; use teach-back to verify understanding. | When were expectations especially clear? | Communicate one expectation with rationale and check understanding. |
| V2-04-01 | Leader as coach | Coaching evokes ownership rather than supplying every answer; distinguish coaching from direction required for safety. | When did someone help me find my own answer? | Agree on a coaching agenda with a colleague. |
| V2-04-02 | Strengths and possibilities | Identify strengths through evidence of successful action, not labels; explore transfer to a new challenge. | What strength is underused on our team? | Gather two examples and design a strength-based stretch task. |
| V2-04-03 | Coaching conversations | Establish agreement, explore current reality, generate possibilities and agree a learner-owned next step. | When do I rush into advice? | Conduct a coaching conversation and request feedback. |
| V2-04-04 | Developing talent | Scaffold stretch assignments, provide feedback, and gradually transfer ownership; avoid favoritism. | Who could grow into a new responsibility? | Build a 30-day development experiment with a team member. |
| V2-05-01 | Understanding conflict | Distinguish task, relationship and structural conflict; identify conditions rather than label people. | When did disagreement lead to improvement? | Map a low-risk conflict without assigning blame. |
| V2-05-02 | Beyond positions | Explore interests, needs and constraints behind positions; validate without conceding every demand. | What need might be hidden beneath my position? | Write competing perspectives and check them with participants. |
| V2-05-03 | Appreciative conflict dialogue | Co-create an affirmative topic, hear each account, find shared aspirations and design behavioral agreements. | What relationship strength can we build on? | Facilitate a voluntary dialogue about a low-risk disagreement. |
| V2-05-04 | Transforming relationships | Address power imbalance, harm, structures and repair; formal reporting overrides informal mediation when required. | What conditions allow conflict to recur? | Establish one shared agreement and a follow-up review. |
| V2-06-01 | Preparing courageous conversations | Clarify facts, impact, standard, desired outcome and appropriate escalation before initiating dialogue. | When have I avoided a necessary conversation? | Draft an observation-impact-inquiry-expectation opening. |
| V2-06-02 | Developmental feedback | Describe behavior and impact specifically; invite perspective and identify a constructive next action. | What feedback helped me grow? | Give timely strengths-based and corrective feedback. |
| V2-06-03 | Compassionate accountability | Empathy and standards coexist; agree actions, support, dates and consequences consistent with policy. | Where have I confused kindness with avoidance? | Develop a documented, fair follow-up agreement. |
| V2-06-04 | Follow-through and repair | Check commitments, recognize improvement, address recurrence and repair trust without promising immunity. | What makes my commitments credible? | Conduct an agreed follow-up and evaluate progress. |
| V2-07-01 | Building and repairing trust | Trust depends on competence, care, consistency and integrity; repair through acknowledgment and changed behavior. | When has someone earned my trust? | Identify and honor one commitment; request feedback. |
| V2-07-02 | Creating belonging | Belonging does not require conformity; make participation accessible and respect differences. | When did I feel genuinely included? | Invite input from an overlooked team member without pressure. |
| V2-07-03 | Encouraging voice and learning | Psychological safety enables interpersonal risk taking, not exemption from standards; respond constructively to concerns. | How do I respond to bad news? | Invite dissent and visibly act on one suggestion. |
| V2-07-04 | Psychologically safe teams | Design predictable routines for speaking up, debriefing and escalating risks; prohibit retaliation. | When does our team learn best? | Pilot a brief learning huddle and gather anonymous feedback where feasible. |
| V2-08-01 | Empowerment and autonomy | Clarify decision rights and boundaries; support independent judgment within competence and policy. | When have I felt trusted to decide? | Transfer one bounded decision and agree check-in points. |
| V2-08-02 | Effective delegation | Agree outcomes, constraints, resources, authority and checkpoints; match challenge to readiness. | What do I hold onto unnecessarily? | Delegate a task with explicit success criteria and support. |
| V2-08-03 | Meaningful recognition | Recognize specific contributions and impact in a manner welcomed by the recipient; avoid inequity. | What recognition mattered most to me? | Ask recognition preferences and acknowledge three concrete contributions. |
| V2-08-04 | Sustaining a Stay Culture | Use Define, Discover, Dream, Design, Destiny to sustain experiments and evidence; avoid attributing turnover changes to training alone. | What is the best of our culture worth amplifying? | Present a 90-day leadership plan with measures, milestones and support. |

## Pathways
Use organization-assigned pathway keys qsr, home_care, dd_services, hospice, home_health, assisted_living, skilled_nursing, senior_living and education (later release). Each shares the 32 competencies, with pathway-specific fictional case, questions, simulation role, assignment and knowledge check. QSR: shift handoffs, young employees, peak rush, guest recovery. Home care: lone-worker support, schedules, family expectations, retention. DD: supported decision-making, dignity, direct support professional development, choice. Hospice: interdisciplinary dialogue, grief, compassion fatigue. Home health: field coordination, clinical boundaries, handoffs. Assisted living: resident autonomy, family communication, care teams. Skilled nursing: speaking up, interdisciplinary staffing, quality and safety. Senior living: hospitality, cross-department teamwork, resident experience. Education: faculty voice, parent relationships, teacher development. No clinical credential or regulatory compliance claims. Urgent safety, abuse, discrimination and mandatory reporting concerns follow organizational policy and applicable law rather than informal coaching.

## Implementation
- Existing academy/learn, academy-manager, dedicated Supabase and invitation/billing flows are the foundation. Inspect live schema/data before changing anything; deployment and launch docs may be stale.
- Create a distinct versioned course with stable V2 lesson IDs, separate enrollments and pathway assignments. Never overwrite legacy course, lesson IDs, enrollment, reflections, submissions or graduation records. Do not execute draft_curriculum_structure.sql against a live course. No automatic completion equivalency; provide opt-in mapping only after review.
- Server verifies organization membership, entitlement, pathway and enrollment ownership. Existing RLS and server-side gates must remain. Use additive migrations and fail closed. Keep education unpublished until authored/tested.
- AI simulation endpoint authenticates learner, validates allowed lesson and pathway, applies length/rate limits, uses server-held API secrets, fictional cases only, and returns structured developmental feedback. No patient/client/employee identifiers; no actual clinical advice, assessment or automated employment decisions. Private reflections and transcripts learner-only by default; explicitly shared assignments separate.
- Individual 360: consent-based exchange of minimal authorized competency summaries, not raw reviewer responses. Verify canonical dimension IDs and cross-project identity; provide manual goal selection until secure integration exists. No automatic organizational access to individual reports.
- Development plan: baseline strengths, one or two growth goals, observable behaviors, support, 30/60/90-day milestones, evidence and review. Recommend courses by goal, not punitive ranking.
- Organization dashboard: invitations, participation and completion; aggregate trends only with sufficient group size; no private reflection or simulation transcript exposure.
- Service pathway: voluntary individual coaching, team coaching, Individual 360, organizational Stay Culture 360, Appreciative Inquiry summits and ongoing OD. Recommendations contextual and optional. Preserve beta and paid entitlements; do not modify pricing or Stripe configuration in this change.
- Release gates: baseline snapshot of legacy row IDs/counts/completion; migration tested in staging; 32 fully authored lessons and distinct contextual overlays; cross-tenant, cross-pathway, privacy, invitation, existing QSR, graduation, progress, mobile, keyboard, AI authorization and billing regression tests. Verify hosted deployment and rollback; never enable billing on test evidence alone.

## Pilot measures
Baseline and follow-up confidence, practice completion, behavior evidence, manager support, voluntary feedback, and aggregate retention indicators where available; no guaranteed ROI claims.

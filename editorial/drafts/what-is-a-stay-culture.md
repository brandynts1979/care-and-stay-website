# What Is a Stay Culture?

An organization can count how many people stayed last year. The number matters. But it cannot, by itself, tell us whether people stayed because they felt respected, because they were growing, because their work had meaning—or because leaving was not yet an option.

Retention is an outcome. Culture is the everyday experience that helps shape it.

At Care & Stay, we use the phrase *Stay Culture* to describe an organization that intentionally creates the conditions in which people can contribute, develop, belong, and choose to remain. The word *choose* matters. A Stay Culture isn't a strategy for persuading people to endure a workplace that isn't working. It is an invitation to create one that is worth being part of.

## Beyond the exit interview

When someone resigns, leaders often ask: Why are you leaving? The question can reveal something important, but it comes late. Consider another question, asked while a person is still here: *When have you felt most energized and valued in your work—and what made that possible?*

That question doesn't ignore frustration. It begins by identifying what deserves to be preserved and multiplied. From there, a manager can ask what gets in the way of having more experiences like that. Appreciation and accountability belong in the same conversation.

This is part of what an appreciative approach makes possible. Rather than treating an organization only as a collection of problems to fix, we can also discover what already works, examine why it works, and build on it.

## Seven connected conditions

Our Stay Culture framework explores seven dimensions of the employee experience:

**Purpose and belonging.** Do people understand how their contribution matters, and do they feel they have a place on the team?

**Trust and safety.** Can people raise concerns, offer ideas, and acknowledge mistakes without unnecessary fear?

**Appreciation.** Is meaningful work noticed, and do people receive recognition that is specific and sincere?

**Growth.** Can people develop skills, assume responsibility, and envision a future?

**Voice and influence.** Are people invited into decisions that affect their work, and can their insights lead to action?

**Sustainable work.** Are expectations, resources, and workloads managed in ways that acknowledge people have lives beyond the job?

**Leadership credibility.** Do leaders behave consistently with the values they name, particularly when decisions become difficult?

These conditions are interconnected. Recognition may feel hollow when workloads remain impossible. An invitation to speak loses force when feedback is routinely ignored. Training alone will not repair broken trust.

A Stay Culture is built from the relationships among these experiences—and from what people encounter day after day.

## Keeping the right people does not mean keeping everyone

A healthy culture needs high expectations, honest feedback, fairness, and room for thoughtful transitions. People sometimes leave for good reasons. A Stay Culture does not promise zero turnover. It aims for a workplace in which people can do meaningful work, develop, and make informed choices about their future.

That also means listening when an employee and a role are no longer a fit. The goal is not loyalty at any cost. It is a healthier relationship between people and the work they do.

## Start with one different conversation

Before launching another initiative, a leader could ask a team member three questions:

1. When has work felt especially meaningful recently?
2. What made that experience possible?
3. What is one thing we could change to make more moments like that possible?

Then listen. Name a practical next step. Return to the conversation and explain what happened.

An assessment can help an organization see broader patterns, but the culture is ultimately lived between people. It is formed in everyday commitments and repeated experiences.

A Stay Culture doesn't begin with a slogan. It begins when leaders become curious about what helps people thrive—and take responsibility for making more of it possible.

*Reflection:* What is one reason your strongest contributors choose to stay—and how sure are you that you know?

What the Quits Rate Can—and Cannot—Tell Us

Care & Stay editorial draft · Byline and publication date pending approval

One number can signal a workforce challenge without explaining the reasons behind it. That is especially important when comparing hospitality and healthcare.

The finding

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey reports that the 2025 annual-average monthly quits rate was 4.2% for accommodation and food services and 2.0% for healthcare and social assistance. These are national, broad-industry averages—not individual employer turnover rates. Source: BLS, JOLTS Table 22, 2025 annual average: BLS JOLTS Table 22.

What the comparison does not prove

A higher quits rate does not demonstrate that leaders in one industry care less about employees. Different job structures, career paths, labor markets, wages, schedules, and worker circumstances can shape movement. The BLS figures measure voluntary separations in the survey, not whether those departures were regrettable or avoidable.

What leaders can do

Review your organization's own voluntary departures alongside tenure, location, role, scheduling practices, and employee experiences. Then ask a more human question: What is helping our people do their best work, and what gets in the way? Treat the national benchmark as a starting point, not a diagnosis.

For your reflection

If your team's quit rate improved tomorrow, what else would you need to know before concluding that your culture had improved?
